# 07 Field Guide: the new restaurant as a place-branding story

Chosen place: a restaurant about ten minutes from home that opened on 1 October 2026 in a unit that previously housed another restaurant. Owner met once in passing; family-run (owner, his wife, their children present). Owners: Hannah and her husband. Interview booked for Monday 19 October; Hannah receives the questions on Sunday 18 October. Name, address and street still to be recorded: [[RESTAURANT NAME]], [[STREET / AREA]].

Working angle: **a brand-new restaurant as a door into its street and neighbourhood.** The restaurant is the character; the place being branded is the area around it. The question the portfolio answers: *what does a new restaurant opening here tell us about this part of Örebro, who it welcomes, and how it is finding its first regulars?*

## 1. Tomorrow: the first approach (five minutes, no interview)

Goal: get a yes and a time. Nothing else.

Say roughly this, in your own words:

> "Hi, I'm David. I live ten minutes from here and I'm a master's student at Örebro University. For a course I'm writing a feature about this part of town, and I'd like to build it around your restaurant, since you've just opened. It would mean one conversation of about 30 to 40 minutes, sometime this week or next, when it's quiet for you. It's a course project, not advertising, and I'd quote you accurately. Would you be open to that?"

Then:
- Fix a **day and time** before you leave. Quiet hours (mid-afternoon) are best. Write it down in front of him.
- Ask for the **correct spelling of his name** and the restaurant's name, and a phone number or Instagram handle to confirm.
- Say one sentence about **photos**: "I'd also like to take a few photographs of the place, with your permission, when I come back." Leave the details for later.
- Buy something small or sit down for a coffee if you can. It is the cheapest way to turn a cold approach into a warm one, and you start your observation notes on the spot.

Do not ask the interview questions tomorrow. If he starts talking, listen, note anything quotable afterwards, and still book the proper sit-down.

If he mentions the earlier confusion (you asking about openings), laugh about it. It is a good line for the feature if he says something about how many people asked when they would open.

## 3. Boxes to tick for a strong piece

Place branding, not a restaurant review. Each box below maps to something the brief or the readings reward.

**A. The place is bigger than the restaurant**
- [ ] You can describe the street in three concrete sentences: what is on it, who walks it, what it looks like at two different times of day
- [ ] You know what was in the unit before, roughly when it closed, and what the owner knows about it
- [ ] At least one person who is *not* the owner or a customer says something about the area (a neighbour, a shopkeeper next door, a bus-stop regular)

**B. One person carries the story**
- [ ] The feature is built around the owner (or his wife, if she is the better talker) as a person with a decision: why here, why now, why this food
- [ ] You have one specific memory from him with a time, a place and a detail (the first customer, the first bad day, the moment the sign went up)

**C. Honesty, with the brief's "what could be better"**
- [ ] One thing you observed that could be better (signage, hours, language, waiting, noise, price clarity)
- [ ] One thing a customer or neighbour said that is not simple praise
- [ ] The owner has been asked what he would change, and his answer is in the notes

**D. The three layers of place communication (Kavaratzis, 2004)**
- [ ] What the place physically *is* and *does*: frontage, interior, menu, prices, hours, staff behaviour (primary)
- [ ] What it *says about itself*: sign, window, Instagram or Facebook, menu language, any flyers (secondary)
- [ ] What *others say*: Google reviews so far, neighbours, word of mouth you overheard (tertiary)
- [ ] You have found at least one gap between those layers. That gap is usually the story.

**E. Newcomer's eyes**
- [ ] You can list what a non-Swedish speaker would hit first: menu language, card or cash, booking, how to order
- [ ] You know how to get there by bus and bike from campus and have the stop name

**F. Evidence discipline**
- [ ] Every quotation in `fieldwork/interview_notes.md` verification log with a timestamp
- [ ] Every fact (opening date, previous business, prices, hours) in `fieldwork/source_log.md`
- [ ] Interviewee's attribution preference written down (full name, first name, or role only)

**G. Four audiences, four different openings**
- [ ] Feature: a scene. Press release: the news (opened 1 October, who, what, where). Webpage: what a resident needs to decide to go. LinkedIn: what a partner gains.
- [ ] Each text tells the reader something the other three do not

**H. Photographs** (details later)
- [ ] Permission from the owner for the interior and for staff
- [ ] Any person recognisable in a photo has agreed. Staged photos of staff are fine if they are staff; do not present posed friends as customers in anything you label as reporting.

## 4. Questions for the owner (pick 10 to 12, in this order)

Superseded on 13 October by the numbered, research-question-tagged list in `08_Plan_and_Questions_for_Lecturer.md` section 9, which is what the lecturer will grade against. The list below is kept as a bank of extra follow-ups.

**Warm-up**
1. Tell me about the first day, 1 October. What do you remember about the first hour?
2. How many people asked you "when are you opening?" before you did? What did that tell you?

**Why here**
3. Why this street, this unit? What did you see when you first walked in?
4. What do you know about the restaurant that was here before? Did people mention it to you?
5. How would you describe this neighbourhood to someone who has never been to Örebro?
6. Who did you expect to come in? Who actually comes in?

**The place he is building**
7. What should a newcomer to the city know before they walk in here?
8. What makes this place special to you, beyond the food?
9. Walk me through a decision you made about the room, the menu or the sign, and why.
10. Running this as a family, with the children around: what does that change about how the place feels?

**The honest part**
11. What has been harder than you expected?
12. What would you change if you could, today?
13. What do people get wrong about this place, or about this area?

**For the other texts**
14. If a local journalist asked you in one sentence why this opening matters for the area, what would you say? (press release quotation)
15. Is there a local organisation, business or group you would like to work with? (LinkedIn angle)
16. What are your hours, how do people book, do you take card, is the menu in English? (webpage facts, confirm against the sign and website)

**Close**
17. Is there anything I have not asked that you think matters?
18. Who else should I talk to, here or on the street?

Follow-ups to keep ready: "Can you give me an example?" "What did that look like?" "What happened next?"

## 5. Questions for customers (three to five minutes each, two or three people)

Approach: "Hi, sorry to interrupt. I'm a master's student at Örebro University writing a feature about this restaurant and the area for a course. Could I ask you two or three quick questions? I can use your first name or no name, whichever you prefer."

1. How did you hear about this place?
2. Is this your first time? If not, what brought you back?
3. Do you live or work around here? How would you describe this street to a friend?
4. What was the first thing you noticed when you walked in?
5. Anything you would tell the owners to change?

Write down: first name (if allowed), roughly their age bracket, whether they are local, and one exact sentence.

## 6. Questions for a neighbour or nearby shopkeeper (one or two people)

1. How long have you been on this street? How has it changed?
2. What was in that unit before, and what happened to it?
3. Have you noticed anything different since the restaurant opened: footfall, noise, who comes by?
4. What does this street need that it does not have?
