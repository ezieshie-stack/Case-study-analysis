# 03 Interview Guide

The feature must be built on at least one interview with someone connected to the place: an organiser, resident, visitor or participant. Aim for 25 to 40 minutes.

## A. Before the interview

- [ ] Choose the person for what they can show readers, not for their title. A regular visitor often gives better material than a manager.
- [ ] Email or ask in person. Say who you are, that it is for a course project at Örebro University (MK009A), what the feature is about, and how long it takes. Offer to meet at the place itself.
- [ ] Prepare 8 to 10 questions, ordered from easy to harder. Memorise the first three so you can keep eye contact.
- [ ] Test the recorder. Bring a notebook anyway.

## B. Consent script (say this at the start)

> "Thanks for making time. I'm a student at Örebro University. This interview is for a writing project in my Content Management course. I'll write a feature story about [[PLACE]] and may quote you by name. Is that OK? Would you prefer first name only, or no name? May I record so I get your words right? You can ask me to stop or to leave something out at any point."

Write down the answers (name use, recording yes/no) at the top of `fieldwork/interview_notes.md`.

## C. Question bank

Pick and adapt. The brief's own four questions are marked with an asterisk.

**Opening (easy, concrete)**
1. How did you first come to [[PLACE]]? What do you remember about that first time?
2. What does a typical visit or working day here look like for you?

**The brief's core questions**
3. * What makes this place special to you?
4. * What should a newcomer know before they come?
5. * Can you describe a memorable experience here?
6. * What could be better?

**Angle-specific (adapt to the chosen angle)**
7. Who do you see coming here that people might not expect?
8. Have you noticed international students or newcomers using the place? What do they seem to come for, and what do they struggle with?
9. What do people get wrong about this place?
10. If you had to explain this place to a friend in one sentence, what would you say? (This often yields a strong quotation.)

**Closing**
11. Is there anything I haven't asked that you think matters?
12. Is there someone else I should talk to? (Second voice for the feature.)

**Follow-up prompts to keep in your pocket:** "Can you give me an example?" "What did that look like?" "What happened next?" "How did that feel?"

## D. During the interview

- Let silences run for three seconds before the next question. People fill them.
- Write down exact phrases that sound like quotations as you hear them, with a timestamp if recording.
- Note the setting: where you are sitting, what the person is doing with their hands, what is going on around you. These details anchor the feature.
- Ask for the spelling of their name and their preferred description (role, how long connected to the place).

## E. After the interview, same day

1. Transcribe the quotations you intend to use, word for word, into the **Quotation verification log** at the bottom of `fieldwork/interview_notes.md`. Record the timestamp or the note page.
2. Summarise the rest of the conversation in your own words.
3. Email a short thank-you and, if you promised, the quotations you plan to use. You are not obliged to let interviewees approve the article, but checking quotations is good practice and protects you.
4. Add the interview to `fieldwork/source_log.md` (date, place, duration, recorded yes/no).

## F. Rules that protect the work

- Never improve a quotation. Tidy "um"s and false starts; do not change meaning or add words.
- Paraphrase without quotation marks when you are unsure of exact wording.
- When translating a quotation, preserve the speaker's meaning rather than the word order, mark it as translated, and have a Swedish speaker check it. If a word could mean two things, ask the speaker, say so, or paraphrase (lecturer, 15 Oct).
- Do not quote anyone who did not consent to being quoted.
- If the interviewee says something critical about an institution, keep it, but give the institution's position from its own material or a second source where you can.
