# 02 Research Guide

Purpose: get to know the place well enough to write accurately for four audiences, and keep a record so every fact can be checked later.

## A. Desk research checklist

Work through this before the visit. Log every source in `fieldwork/source_log.md`.

**The place's own communication (secondary communication in Kavaratzis's terms: what the place says about itself on purpose)**
- [ ] Official website: what it says it is, who it says it is for, opening hours, prices, accessibility, languages offered
- [ ] Social media accounts: last 20 posts. What do they show, who appears, what tone, which language(s)
- [ ] Printed material or signage visible on site (photograph it)
- [ ] Any mission statement, annual report, or municipality page about it

**Independent sources (tertiary communication: what others say)**
- [ ] Local news coverage in the last two years (NA, SVT Örebro, Sveriges Radio P4 Örebro)
- [ ] Reviews and visitor comments (Google, TripAdvisor, student forums). Note recurring praise and recurring complaints
- [ ] Örebro University pages or student union material that mention the place
- [ ] Wikipedia or municipal history pages for dates and background. Confirm key dates in a second source

**Context**
- [ ] Who runs it (municipality, association, company, volunteers), and how it is funded if public
- [ ] How to get there by bus, bike and on foot from the university and from the centre
- [ ] What else is nearby that a first-time visitor would combine it with

## B. Questions the desk research should answer

1. What does the place claim to be and who does it claim to serve?
2. What do visitors actually say, and where do the claims and the comments disagree?
3. What has changed recently (new programme, renovation, funding, controversy)? This is the likeliest press-release angle.
4. What would a newcomer with no Swedish get stuck on (language, hours, booking, cost)?
5. What is the one detail nobody mentions online that a visit could reveal?

## C. Visit: observation protocol

Spend at least 60 to 90 minutes. Use `fieldwork/observation_notes.md`.

Record, in this order, as you notice them:
- **Arrival:** how you found it, signage, first impression in the first 30 seconds, who is at the door
- **People:** rough count, apparent mix (ages, groups, solo visitors, families, students), what they are doing
- **Sound, light, smell, temperature:** one line each. These are the details that make feature writing concrete (Friedmann's point about writing for the senses and showing rather than telling)
- **Interactions:** staff with visitors, visitors with each other. Note any moment of welcome and any moment of confusion
- **Language:** what languages you hear and read, whether English material exists
- **Friction:** anything that would stop or slow a newcomer (closed doors, cash only, Swedish-only forms, no seating)
- **One memorable scene:** a 3 to 5 sentence description of one specific moment, with time. This often becomes the feature's lead or ending
- **Photos:** list what you photographed and whether people are identifiable (ask before photographing people)

Write up the notes within 24 hours while memory is fresh. Mark anything you are unsure of with `(?)`.

## D. Fact-checking rule

A fact goes into a text only if it has a source entry in `fieldwork/source_log.md` or an observation note with a time. Quotations follow the interview guide's verification log. If a fact cannot be confirmed, either cut it or write it as what someone said, attributed.
