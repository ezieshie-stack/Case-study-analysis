## 3. Audience choices

**Feature.** Audience: international students and other newcomers to Örebro. Platform: The Local Sweden. [[Why the story matters to them, and why a feature of about 800 to 1,200 words.]]

> ✎ Smith (Becoming a Public Relations Writer) on the feature: built around people, with an engaging lead, a nut graph, a body of quotations and observation, and an ending that returns to a detail. Friedmann (Writing for Visual Media): concrete, sensory, scene-based writing.

**Reusing one message and one photograph.** [[Two or three sentences: the core message and the photo you reused, and why consistency across platforms matters for a place brand.]]

> ✎ Kavaratzis: coherent communication is part of building a place brand. The lecturer asked you to show how one picture and one message can be adapted.

**Why these three platforms.** [[One sentence: you chose from Swedish usage data rather than your own habits, because platform habits differ between countries.]]

**Instagram.** Audience: students at Örebro University. Purpose: introduce the people behind the restaurant. [[Why a photo-first post with a short caption suits them.]]

> ✎ Evidence: 69% of Swedes born in the 1990s and 65% born in the 2000s use Instagram daily (Internetstiftelsen, 2025). Friedmann: the image shows, the caption adds what the image cannot.

**Facebook.** Audience: residents of the neighbourhood around Karlsdalsallén. Purpose: tell neighbours who has opened in the old premises and invite them in. [[Why more text and practical detail suit them, and why a Swedish version would be used in practice.]]

> ✎ Evidence: Internetstiftelsen (2025) heads its age chapter "Facebook dominerar bland äldre"; 62 to 64% of Swedes born in the 1950s to 1980s use it daily, against 25% of those born in the 2000s.

**LinkedIn.** Audience: Örebro's local business community and people considering starting a business. Purpose: share what starting a local business has been like. [[What these readers gain and what the call to action is.]]

> ✎ Evidence: 42% of Swedes born in the 1970s to 1990s used LinkedIn in the past year, and its use rose from 25% in 2021 to 29% in 2025 (Internetstiftelsen, 2025). Smith: persuasive writing for a professional public, benefit first, one clear next step.
