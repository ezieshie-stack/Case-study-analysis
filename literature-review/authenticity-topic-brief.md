## 1. Working title

> **The Paradox of Strategic Authenticity: A Critical Review of Authenticity in Strategic Organisational Communication**

Alternatives considered: *Performing the Real*; *Can Authenticity Be Strategic?*

---

## 2. The problem (for the introduction)

Authenticity is one of the most sought-after qualities in contemporary organisational
communication, yet it rests on an apparent contradiction. Authenticity implies
genuineness — being what one claims to be, without calculation. Strategic
communication is by definition intentional: the purposeful use of communication by an
organisation to achieve its mission (Hallahan et al., 2007). An organisation that
*plans* to appear authentic has, in one obvious sense, already compromised the quality
it is pursuing.

The tension sharpens where internal and external communication meet. Organisations
increasingly ask employees to "live the brand" and act as brand ambassadors, while
simultaneously presenting the organisation to external publics as authentic. If
internal experience contradicts the external claim, what happens to the claim — and
what happens to the employee?

## 3. Purpose statement

> The purpose of this review is to map and critically synthesise how research in
> strategic and organisational communication conceptualises organisational
> authenticity, and to examine how this literature accounts for the tension between
> authenticity as genuineness and strategic communication as intentional, managed
> meaning-making — with particular attention to the relationship between internal and
> external authenticity claims.

**Note on genre.** The paradox is the *problem statement*, not the purpose. Framing the
purpose as a yes/no question ("can the two coexist?") would produce an argumentative
essay; the rubric grades the ability to "define a research area within strategic
communication and organize and discuss research findings within it." The paradox
should emerge as a *finding* — a fault line running through the field — rather than as
an assumption.

## 5. Organising principle for the Results section

Appendix C(d) requires a declared and justified organising principle. Proposal:
organise by **where each strand of the literature locates authenticity**.

1. **Authenticity as organisational property** (essentialist) — consistency with
   heritage, origin and stated values. *Napoli et al. (2014); Beverland.*
2. **Authenticity as audience judgment** (receiver-side) — authenticity as something
   attributed by publics, and therefore measurable. *Morhart et al. (2015);
   Molleda (2010); Lehman et al. (2019).*
3. **Authenticity as communicative performance** (performative/discursive) —
   authenticity as produced *in* communication. *Christensen, Morsing & Thyssen (2013);
   Chen & Eriksson (2019); Banet-Weiser (2012).*
4. **Authenticity as control** (critical) — authenticity as a technique of normative
   control over employees. *Fleming & Sturdy (2009); M. Müller (2017);
   Alvesson & Einola (2019).*

**Why this principle:** strands 1–2 treat the paradox as a solvable problem (be
consistent, and you will be perceived as authentic); strands 3–4 treat it as
constitutive and unresolvable. The organising principle therefore *produces* the
paradox as a result rather than presupposing it.

**Cross-cutting thread:** the internal–external relationship. M. Müller (2017) is the
hinge — brand-centred control recruits the *external* audience as an additional source
of *internal* normative control.

**Ethical dimension** (Appendix C(h)) is intrinsic: emotional labour, the erosion of
the boundary between work and private life, and the distinction between deception and
aspiration.

## 6. Delimitation (Appendix C(b))

Ten pages cannot hold the whole authenticity literature. Include and exclude
explicitly, with justification:

**Include:** organisational-level authenticity in strategic communication, public
relations, corporate communication, internal communication and branding;
approx. 2005–2025.

**Exclude, with stated reasons:**
- Authenticity of products, places, tourism, food and heritage (different referent)
- Individual/existential authenticity in psychology
- Authentic *leadership* psychometrics — represented by its critique
  (Alvesson & Einola, 2019) rather than surveyed in full
- Consumer scale-validation studies beyond one or two representative examples

## 11. Route to the spring thesis

The course guide notes that a well-chosen topic can seed the Master's thesis. Candidate
directions from this review:

- Multimodal critical discourse analysis of one organisation's authenticity claims,
  read against employee accounts (methodologically close to Chen & Eriksson, 2019)
- Interview study with communication practitioners on how they manage the demand to
  produce authenticity
- Study of the internal–external axis directly: how employees experience being asked
  to embody a brand identity they may not recognise
